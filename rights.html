Rights & takedown

Everything HeOCR publishes is meant to be usable by anyone, for any purpose, permanently. This page sets out how we check that, and what to do if you think we got something wrong.

What we use

  • Public-domain works only. A book is used only if every author, translator and editor whose work is on the page died more than 70 years ago, which makes it public domain in Israel. Books printed in or before 1855 with no known death year are presumed public domain. US status is recorded for each item as well, so a US-only release can be built.
  • Faithful scans. A faithful photographic or mechanical scan of a printed page creates no new copyright in Israel or the US, so the scan is as free as the book.
  • Sources we have no restrictive agreement with.
    • National Library of Israel scans marked "public domain, free use";
    • public-domain scans fetched from the Internet Archive or Wikimedia Commons;
    • texts from Project Ben-Yehuda;
    • fonts under the SIL Open Font License.
  • What we leave out. Anything under non-commercial or no-derivatives terms, or with unknown terms. That includes National Library of Israel scans marked "public domain, non-commercial use", unless the library allows otherwise in writing.

A record for every item

Every scan we use carries a rights record, generated from evidence. Every line, letter or page cut from that scan points back to it. The record gives:

  • where the file came from, with its exact URL and checksum;
  • who wrote the work and when they died, when and where it was printed, and the sources for each of these;
  • who made the scan, such as a library or Google, and any conditions the original holder attached;
  • why the item is free to reuse, with a one-line summary.

If a required fact is missing, the item is left out. We credit the National Library of Israel on every item from its collection, and we remove Google's cover pages and watermarks from Google-digitized scans while keeping that fact in the record.

Licences

  • Scans, line images and letter images: public domain, marked CC0 1.0. HeOCR claims no copyright in them.
  • HeOCR's own data (alignments, annotations, synthetic pages): CC0 1.0.
  • Models: Apache-2.0.
  • Code: MIT.
  • Hebrew Wikisource text, used for evaluation, keeps its CC BY-SA licence.

Takedown

If you believe an item is under copyright or under a binding restriction, please tell us:

  1. Open an issue at github.com/HeOCR/hocrgen/issues with the title Takedown: <item or book id>, and the legal basis if you know it.
  2. We withdraw the affected book from public downloads straight away, while we look into it.
  3. If it has to go, it is removed from every dataset and listed in a public changelog. Because each item keeps its own record, this takes minutes and leaves the rest of the data untouched.
