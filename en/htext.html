htext

Openly licensed Hebrew text for training HeOCR's recognizers, cut into lines, plain and with niqqud: public-domain text first, and attribution and share-alike text in their own releases. Every work or document carries its own rights record: its status in Israel and in the US, its licence and its credit line. htext is published as three releases, one per licence, so you can pick the terms you need; the core release is CC0 and can be used without conditions.

Updated 2026-11-27. Figures come from each corpus card and are rebuilt from corpora.json.

What htext is

htext collects Hebrew text whose rights are clear enough to publish openly, and turns it into training lines for hocrsyngen, HeOCR's synthetic page generator. The generator renders each line onto synthetic pages, so the recognizers learn real Hebrew vocabulary, spelling and punctuation, not only made-up text.

Each source has its own builder, its own corpus and its own card. A build reads a pinned copy of the source, computes rights per entry, cleans and cuts the text, and checks that no line overlaps HeOCR's evaluation text. Only the files a card lists are published.

Rights

Every entry has a rights record with two fields, pd_israel and pd_us, each with the basis it was computed from and the year it holds for. A corpus variant includes an entry only if its record passes that variant's rule. The records are published with each corpus.

A translation counts as public domain only if both its author and its translator are. Every entry also records its source licence and credit line, so each release can be rebuilt and filtered. No release takes text under a non-commercial (NC) or no-derivatives (ND) licence, and text never crosses releases: a CC0 build never contains a CC BY or CC BY-SA line.

The rights fields are the project's reading of the law, not legal advice.

  • Project Ben-Yehuda: Public domain in Israel: every author and every translator died at least 71 years before the build year (2026), with death years from Wikidata or, where Wikidata has none, from a reviewed source such as the author's PBY page. US status is recorded per work: public domain in the US when the transcribed edition appeared before 1931.
  • The Knesset of Israel: Laws are not protected by copyright in Israel (section 6 of the Copyright Act 2007). US status is not assessed.
  • Westminster Leningrad Codex, via the Open Scriptures Hebrew Bible: The Leningrad Codex is about a thousand years old, and the WLC text is in the public domain ("The text of the WLC remains in the Public Domain", OSHB). Public domain in Israel and in the US. Only the words are used; OSHB's CC BY lemma and morphology layers are not. The edition is WLC, not UXLC.

Download and use

Use the release links above to download the text files. The corpus cards and SHA-256 checksums are in the source repository. Every file is UTF-8 with one line per row, NFC normalised, with no blank lines.

Download the files and check them against the published checksums:

gh release download knesset-laws-v2 -R HeOCR/htext
curl -LO https://raw.githubusercontent.com/HeOCR/htext/main/data/knesset_laws/v2/SHA256SUMS
grep -E '_v2\.txt$' SHA256SUMS | sha256sum -c

Use a file as training text for hocrsyngen, which records it as external:sha256:<hex>:

hocrsyngen ... --text-corpus knesset_laws_lines_plain_v2.txt

Or rebuild a corpus from its source: each corpus has a build guide in the repository, for example the PBY guide.
