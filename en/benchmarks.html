P-mod-open v0 results

31 pages · 65 images · 1,863 reference lines · Download the benchmark dataset · Cite the dataset (Zenodo DOI)

Official gazettes, legal notices and other public documents in modern unpointed Hebrew. A scanner and two phones captured the same 17 printed pages, scored against the same text for each page. Other images come from archives or publishers’ PDFs.

The primary metric is page character error rate (CER).

HeOCR modern MEASURED v1.0 ties Rivok Full on modern printed documents (14.77% vs 14.80% CER in the recommended view). That is not a win under our registered test. It is clearly better than stock Tesseract.

PBY-H v0 results

36 pages · 12 works · 1,017 scored lines

Pages from public-domain Hebrew literary works in Project Ben-Yehuda, imaged from scans of the exact printed editions. The text has no vowel marks.

The primary metric is page character error rate (CER).

HeOCR literature-measured v1.0 (7.03% CER) has the lowest error on this set. It is better than Google Cloud Vision (narrowly), Google Document AI, Tesseract and Rivok PaddleOCR, and statistically level with Rivok Tesseract Lite, Rivok Tesseract Full and our previous model, HeOCR old-print v1.1. This is not a registered state-of-the-art result.

PBY-H v0.1 pointed results

24 pages · 8 works · 391 scored lines

Pages from public-domain pointed Hebrew works (poetry) in Project Ben-Yehuda, imaged from Internet Archive scans of two printed editions. The reference is Project Ben-Yehuda’s typed text, with the vowel points as typed there.

The primary metric is page character error rate (CER) with vowel points kept.

Google leads on this set: Google Document AI (9.50% CER) and Google Cloud Vision (9.67%). HeOCR pointed-measured v1.0 (17.38%) is better than our previous model, HeOCR old-print v1.1 (19.12%), and statistically level with Rivok Tesseract Full. Its gain over v1.1 is in the vowel points: on letters alone the two are level. This is not a registered state-of-the-art result.
